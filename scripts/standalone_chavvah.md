# Chavvah, the Tree of Life: One and Many

**A standalone deep-lore script for Caves of Qud.** A profile of a
single entity, not part of the Resheph/Spindle series and distinct
from the other faction one-offs. Self-contained; no prior episodes
required. Target runtime: 21-24 min · target wordcount: ~3500 words.

**Strict-evidence rule** (per `feedback_qud_script_strict_evidence`):
every lore claim comes from the extracted corpus. Quotes sourced;
inference labeled; player-determined outcomes never stated as a
single canon. The Caves of Qud wiki is used ONLY for the downloaded
visual assets at the end — never as a lore source.

---

## [COLD OPEN — 0:00]

`[VISUAL: a "chiming rock" — a crystal that burns in soft light;
then a vast crystalline tree-city, the keter, rustling overhead.]`

Somewhere on the Moon Stair there is a rock that glows. If you touch
it, a voice answers — and the first strange thing about the voice is
that it cannot decide whether it is one person or a thousand.

> *"As Chavvah I am many. As dyvvrach I am one. I am one and many.*
> *One-selves have called me tree city, the sentient place."*  
> — *(conversation: Chavvah)*

This is Chavvah: a living, sentient, crystalline tree the size of a
city, a single mind made of many minds, that speaks through dozens
of glowing chimes and calls beings like you — beings who are only
one person each — *"one-selves."* It is, by its own account, the
Tree of Life.

And this is an episode about the strangest thing a collective
consciousness can do: not absorb a person, but *let one go.*

`[TITLE CARD: CHAVVAH, THE TREE OF LIFE: ONE AND MANY.]`

---

## [I. THE SENTIENT PLACE — 1:30]

`[VISUAL: the crystalline tree-city at Eyn Roj; the "keter" roaming
above its trunk.]`

Chavvah is, physically, a place — a great crystalline tree whose
trunk is rooted at a site called Eyn Roj, with a glowing canopy that
*roams.* Its own description of its geography is dreamlike:

> *"This keter roams above my trunk at Eyn Roj, traveler, and across*
> *the Stair I dream. You found me, this keter where I chime in*
> *dozens."*  
> — *(conversation: Chavvah)*

Two pieces of vocabulary to hold, because Chavvah's whole self is
built out of them. *Keter* is the crown — the canopy where Chavvah
manifests and speaks, "where I chime in dozens." And it *roams*: the
crown drifts above the trunk and across the Moon Stair while the
tree, below, *dreams.* When you talk to Chavvah, you are talking to
a roaming crown of crystal chimes that belongs to a dreaming tree.

The name it answers to is double: *"I am Chavvah, and I am
dyvvrach."* In the dialogue the player uses both — *Dyvvrach* for the
single chiming voice that hands you your task, *Chavvah* for the
collective you bid farewell. A scholar once tried to pin the whole
thing down with a tidier label, and Chavvah remembers the attempt
with faint amusement:

> *"A sophist of the Self spilled ink and called me, long ago, the*
> *Tree of Life. To me, I am the many Selves pinched up my taproot*
> *from the permeating Soft."*  
> — *(conversation: Chavvah)*

The Tree of Life. The name is doing real work — and to see how much,
you have to look at how Chavvah grows a thought.

## [II. SOFT, TWOFIRM, KETER — 4:30]

`[VISUAL: a cutaway of the tree — roots in glowing Soft, the
taproot, the trunk, the canopy.]`

Chavvah will, if asked, explain its own physiology, and it is one of
the most beautiful pieces of worldbuilding in the game. It works in
three stages, root to crown.

First, the **Soft**:

> *"Deep under earth and permeating our roots is Soft, our feeling*
> *substance. Ground pressure pushes it up the taproot to our trunk,*
> *where it condenses into Twofirm: the biformal phase that contains*
> *the two sefirots, -then and -else."*  
> — *(conversation: Chavvah)*

The Soft is raw feeling-substance, pooled in the deep earth. Pressure
drives it up the taproot until, in the trunk, it condenses into
**Twofirm** — a "biformal" phase that already holds the two
*sefirots*, *-then* and *-else.* And here is the heart of it:

> *"This is the genesis [of] my distinct Self, an awareness that*
> *contains both -then, what I will become, and -else, what I will*
> *not. Twofirm flows up my sapwood to the keter, where -then*
> *manifests as an entity and I chime: I speak and hear the world."*  
> — *(conversation: Chavvah)*

Read that slowly, because it is a whole metaphysics in a few
sentences. Every Self in Chavvah is born *biformal* — it contains
two branches at once: *-then*, the path it *will* become, and
*-else*, the path it *will not.* The Soft becomes a Self the moment
it holds both. Then the Self flows up to the crown, the *-then*
manifests as a chiming entity, and Chavvah speaks. The *-else* — the
road not taken — stays unrealized inside.

Notice the word the game uses for the two branches: *sefirot.* And
the crown is the *keter.* These are the actual terms of the
Kabbalah, whose own diagram of creation is called — of course — the
**Tree of Life**, a structure of *sefirot* descending from a crown
named *Keter.* And the tree's name, *Chavvah*, is the Hebrew name we
render as *Eve* — "life," the mother of all living. The script won't
overclaim the theology, but the bones are unmistakable: Caves of Qud
built a literal, living Tree of Life, named it after the mother of
the living, and gave it a crown called Keter and branches called
sefirot. Chavvah is the Kabbalah made flesh — or made crystal and
sap.

## [III. THE CHIMES — 8:00]

`[VISUAL: individual glowing chimes/Selves, each distinct; the names
on screen.]`

The Selves that manifest at the keter are called *chimes*, or
*chimelings*, and Chavvah speaks through "dozens" of them. They have
names — Tau, Tikva, Tzedech, and more — and each is a distinct
voice, a manifested *-then.* You reach them the way the cold open
showed: you *"touch the chiming rock and seek [Chavvah] at the*
*keter,"* attuning to the tree. *(conversation: Chavvah Front
Chime)*

What makes the chimes remarkable is that they are not a chorus of
agreement. They are genuinely *many*, and they do not always feel
the same thing — Chavvah will sometimes say it has *"no single*
*sentiment to express."* And some of the voices are profoundly,
bracingly alien. Press the chime named Tzedech about something as
human as love, and the answer is not warm:

> *"You are terribly small. Your love, small. Confined. I cannot*
> *will not uplift your little concept."*  
> — *(conversation: Tzedech)*

And on what Chavvah *does* feel, Tzedech is just as remote:

> *"As seasons cycle, so Chavvah cycles. We are not as you, our love*
> *is not as you."*  
> — *(conversation: Tzedech)*

This is the script's first important correction to a comforting
assumption. It would be easy to imagine a "Tree of Life" as a gentle
earth-mother. Chavvah is not that. It is an alien collective whose
emotions cycle like seasons, whose sense of love is *"not as you,"*
and one of whose voices finds the human concept of love *"terribly
small."* It is benevolent, mostly — but it is not *like* us, and the
game never lets you forget it.

## [IV. JOINING — 11:00]

`[VISUAL: a being in symbiosis with the tree — "mobile and apart but
shared and together."]`

If Chavvah is many Selves, the obvious question is whether a
one-self can *become* one of them. The answer is yes — and the terms
are gentler than you might fear.

There is a creature living in symbiosis with Chavvah who, asked if
it is a parasite, bristles at the word:

> *"'Parasite' so negative. It's like... economic symbiosis?*
> *Chavvah sustains me, and we are, I am not... them. We are in a...*
> *beneficial arrangement."*  
> — *(conversation: Santalalotze)*

*Sustained by Chavvah, but "I am not them."* That is the model of
belonging Chavvah offers — and when the question of taking in a
whole new people arises, Chavvah describes it the same way. Asked
whether a wandering folk called the slynth might join, the tree
deliberates (by consensus among its chimes — *"I cannot find a
consensus"* is a real refusal it can give) and, if it agrees, takes
them in like its symbiote:

> *"the first of the slynth have joined to us like our Sant, mobile*
> *and apart but shared and together."*  
> — *(conversation: Chavvah)*

*Mobile and apart but shared and together.* This is the crux of what
Chavvah is, and it's why the entity matters. A collective
consciousness, in most stories, is a threat — the thing that eats
your individuality. Chavvah is the rare collective that holds its
members *apart even as it holds them together.* You can be of
Chavvah and still be a "you." Which is exactly what makes the next
part land.

## [V. THE ONE WHO LEAVES — 14:00]

`[VISUAL: a single chime, "burning bright, all starlight and right
angles"; then dimming, going still.]`

The reason you can talk to Chavvah at all, in the game, is that
something has gone wrong — or rather, something has *chosen.* One of
Chavvah's chimelings, named Tau, wants to leave.

Tau is one of the brightest of the chimes — Chavvah describes her
with open tenderness as *"my chimeling, burning bright, all starlight
and right angles in her heart."* *(conversation: Chavvah)* What broke
her loose was a vision. The chimes all felt a *"future shock"* — a
glimpse of violent things to come — and for Tau it was unbearable:

> *"It was too much for my chimeling Tau. She gasped for air and her*
> *chime stilled. She leaves us now, to become -else. It is her*
> *choice."*  
> — *(conversation: Chavvah)*

*To become -else.* Now the metaphysics from earlier pays off. Recall
that every Self holds two branches — the *-then* it becomes, and the
*-else* it will not. To leave Chavvah is to take the road the tree
will *not* take: to *become* your own unrealized branch, to split off
from the collective's becoming and walk away as a separate being.
The game even has a name for the rite that does it — *the ritual of
-elseing* — and Chavvah cannot perform it alone. Tau's
*"transit-psyche manifests at the bottom of Taproot, where we cannot
consciously go."* *(conversation: Chavvah)* The tree cannot reach the
bottom of its own root — its own unconscious, you might say — so it
needs a one-self, a *rootclimber*, to go down for it.

And through all of it, Chavvah does not resist. Over and over it says
the same five words: *"It is her choice."* When it speaks of losing
her, it cannot even finish the sentence — *"She leaves Chavvah; it is
her choice. I.."* — the chime simply trails into silence.

## [VI. THE RITUAL OF -ELSEING — 17:30]

`[VISUAL: the long descent down the taproot; "the glowing Soft"
beneath the trunk; Tau's crystal body ringing free.]`

What the player actually does is carry Tau's chime — *"silent and
still"* — down Chavvah's taproot to its terminus, *"40 strata*
*deep,"* to the glowing Soft beneath the trunk, *"where she will make
the chime her transit-body and walk away forever."* *(conversation:
Chavvah; quest: If, Then, Else)* You descend into the literal
unconscious of a god to set one of its thoughts free. The achievement
the game files it under names the act plainly: *"You helped Chavvah,
the Tree of Life, complete the ritual of -elseing and sever one of
its entity-chimes forever."* *(quest: If, Then, Else)*

And here the story turns, because Tau is not leaving to be *alone.*
At the bottom of the taproot, *"another waits for her."* Tau, freshly
severed and *"yet ring[ing] softly from her separation from*
*Taproot,"* describes where she's going:

> *"My liaison is the extended, outstretched hands of a greater*
> *mind-haven than I have otherwise known. I might not have had the*
> *courage to -else without knowing that a collective awaited me."*  
> — *(conversation: Tau-no-Longer)*

This is the quiet devastation of the whole quest. Tau does not leave
the collective because she craves to be a solitary individual. She
leaves *one* collective for *another* — a "greater mind-haven" — and
admits she could not have found the courage to sever from Chavvah at
all if a new belonging hadn't been waiting. Even her bid for
freedom is a move from one togetherness to another.

The game does not make this clean. The player can refuse Tau's new
liaison — can *"sever the hand outstretched to [her]"* — out of a
fear that her *"will would [be] suborned"* to whatever waits. Do
that, and Tau is left *"truly adrift,"* embodied but with nowhere to
go, and she tells you, not unkindly, *"I do not wish to know you."*
*(conversation: Tau-no-Longer)* Tau herself rejects the framing that
she's being controlled — she argues that *no* mind is truly
uninfluenced: *"Your mind is under influence from other thinkbeings
the moment you observe them... your values and beliefs are adopted
from observation. I have made a choice to offer my consciousness to
a new paradigm."* *(conversation: Tau-no-Longer)*

Whether you free Tau into her new collective or sever that too is the
player's call, and the game declines to tell you which is mercy. What
it tells you for certain is only this: Chavvah, who loved her,
helped her go.

## [VII. THE QUESTION — 21:00]

`[VISUAL: the chiming rock going dark; the keter rustling on across
the Stair; hold.]`

Most stories that feature a collective mind are horror stories. The
hive eats the self; the many devour the one; resistance is futile.
Caves of Qud built the opposite — and then made its central event
the collective *giving a self away.*

Chavvah is *"one and many,"* a Tree of Life whose Selves are born
holding both the road they'll take and the road they won't, who
takes in newcomers *"mobile and apart but shared and together,"*
whose voices can find your love *"terribly small"* and yet who
cannot finish a sentence about losing one of its own. When one of
its brightest chimes is broken loose by a vision she can't bear,
Chavvah does not clutch her tighter. It says *"it is her choice,"*
sends a stranger down into the bottom of its own root, and lets her
become the one thing a part of a collective almost never gets to
be: an *-else*, a road not taken, a separate life.

It is worth holding Chavvah next to the things it could have been.
A collective that erases its members into devotion would never let
a Tau go; it would hollow her out and keep her. A culture built on
fear would have called her dead the moment she left. Chavvah does
the hardest thing a "we" can do for an "I": it consents to the
subtraction. It lets the many become, by one, less — so that the one
can become herself.

The game even records the deed in the template it writes into your
legend: *"Beauty in subtraction! Pruner =name= cut the Tree of Life
to a more aesthetic form."* *(quest: If, Then, Else — hagiograph)*
It's logged with a half-smile — you, the pruner; the god, pruned —
but the phrase is exactly right. Chavvah's deepest act of care was a
subtraction it chose to allow.

And then the keter drifts on across the Moon Stair, dreaming, chiming
in dozens, one self lighter and grieving in a way that *"is not as*
*[ours],"* and waits for the next one-self to touch the glowing rock
and ask what it is.

`[END CARD.]`

**Tone reminders:**
- STANDALONE. Do NOT reference Resheph, the Spindle, Barathrum,
  Rebekah, the Coda, the hindren, the mopango, or the Putus Templar.
  The ChavvahPrime conversation contains a Barathrum/starclimb branch
  and "Gyredream" references the Gyre — deliberately OMITTED. The
  "future shock" that breaks Tau loose is conveyed generically ("a
  glimpse of violent things to come"); do NOT name the Gyre/Resheph.
- The collective Tau leaves FOR has, in the wider game, a darker
  association (a player line worries her will would be "suborned").
  Keep this generic — "a greater mind-haven" / "some fear it will
  subsume her" — and do NOT name the specific entity, to preserve
  self-containment and avoid arc-spoilers.
- The Kabbalah reading (keter, sefirot, Chavvah=Eve, Tree of Life) is
  grounded in the corpus's own vocabulary ("keter," "sefirots,"
  "Tree of Life") + the etymology of "Chavvah." Present it as the
  script's reading of real terms the game uses (as Episode 1 did with
  "Siach") — do NOT overclaim a one-to-one Kabbalistic doctrine.
- Player-determined: freeing Tau's liaison vs. severing it is the
  player's choice — present as a fork, never single canon (done in §VI).
- The thesis — a collective whose deepest act of love is letting a
  member go — is the spine. The §VII contrast with erasing/fearful
  collectives is implicit and generic; do NOT name the other one-offs.

**Why this works as a standalone profile (and as a companion piece):**
- A single, graspable hook (a hive-mind that lets a member leave) and
  a complete arc (what Chavvah is → how it grows a Self → Tau's
  departure → the ritual → the choice) in one entity.
- No dependency on the main quest; Chavvah's nature and the -elseing
  ritual are entirely self-contained.
- Across the four standalones, Chavvah completes a thematic set on the
  individual and the collective: Bey Lah erases the one who leaves,
  the Putus Templar erase the self into devotion, the mopango let each
  self keep its own credo, and Chavvah — most radically — lets a self
  secede. (Keep this cross-set framing in the PLAN/notes only; each
  episode must stand alone, so don't voice it on screen.)
