# Sparafucile: The Finest Gunsmith

**A standalone character-study script for Caves of Qud.** A portrait of
the Barathrumite enclave's master gunsmith — a craftsman who is deaf and
mute, who speaks entirely in gesture and expression, and who his neighbor
describes as beaming "like the high salt sun" at a perfect barrel. Self-
contained; no prior episodes required. Target runtime: 18-20 min · target
wordcount: ~2800 words.

**Strict-evidence rule** (per `feedback_qud_script_strict_evidence`):
every lore claim comes from the extracted corpus or the decompile.
Quotes sourced; inference labeled; player-determined outcomes never
stated as single canon. The wiki is used ONLY for downloaded visual

## [COLD OPEN — 0:00]

`[VISUAL: a masterwork carbine, then a chain pistol; then the overworld
map of Grit Gate half-buried in rock.]`

When you first arrive at Grit Gate — the home and laboratory of the
Barathrumite community — the enclave's steward meets you and offers a
reward. What you receive, in one version of that meeting, is a finely
made firearm. And the steward's words for it are plain:

> *"It bears the mark of our finest gunsmith, Sparafucile."*  
> — *(conversation: Otho)*

So you go looking for this person. You find him at his workbench,
surrounded by papers and scraps and tools. In one greeting he lifts one
brow, giving you a wary look. *(conversation: Sparafucile)* And he says
nothing.

He will never, in any conversation you have with him, say a single word
aloud.

Not because he is taciturn. Not because he distrusts you. Because he
can't speak. And he can't hear.

The finest gunsmith in Grit Gate is deaf and mute. This is an episode
about what it means to be known through what you make, and what it means
to speak entirely in gesture — and about why that, in this particular
community, adds up to something complete.

`[TITLE CARD: SPARAFUCILE — THE FINEST GUNSMITH.]`

## [I. THE MARK — 1:30]

`[VISUAL: Sparafucile's tile; then Hortensa's tile; then a carbine and
chain pistol side by side.]`

Let's start with the weapon, because it comes first. The masterwork
carbine or chain pistol that the steward places in your hands is the
enclave's acknowledgment that you've done something difficult and come
back. And its quality is not generic. It "bears the mark" of a named
person. This is not a storeroom piece. It is a made thing, with a maker.

When you find that maker at their workbench, here is how his neighbor
describes what it looks like when the work goes right:

> *"Oh, I have such a precious vantage point on my sweet neighbor! When*
> *he achieves a moment of perfection in his craft — a flawlessly spiraled*
> *barrel, a mound of powder measured to the grain — he beams like the*
> *high salt sun. What a lovely smile."*  
> — *(conversation: Hortensa)*

A flawlessly spiraled barrel. A mound of powder measured to the grain.
Hortensa is a liquid-tinkerer — by her own description, someone who
thinks of herself as "a wisewoman in dialogue" with matter rather than
a mere experimenter. *(conversation: Hortensa)* She chooses for her
description of Sparafucile not a technical assessment but an emotional
one: he *beams*. The moment of craft perfection is readable on his face
before he even turns it toward you. And she calls her vantage point
*precious* — the seat of the neighbor who has the privilege of watching
excellence land.

The masterwork weapon's quality does not come from nowhere. It comes from
taking the thing seriously enough that getting it right is a physical joy.
From measuring until the measurement is exact. From knowing — by feel, by
eye, by the angle of a spiral — when the barrel is ready and when it is
not.

Sparafucile is the enclave's artisan: the one whose gift is not the
discovery of principles but the making of objects. The finest gunsmith.
The one whose mark is on the first good thing Grit Gate gives you.

## [II. THE SILENCE — 5:00]

`[VISUAL: Sparafucile's tile; then a slow push on his face; then the
enclave's interior.]`

The first thing Sparafucile does when you greet him formally — when you
say "Greetings, Sparafucile" — is raise "one long-fingered hand in
greeting." *(conversation: Sparafucile)* No words. That is the
conversation from the start. From there, the full exchange runs through
twenty-three nodes of narration describing gesture, expression, and
silence. Sparafucile nods, frowns, shrugs, smiles, stares. He produces
charcoal drawings. He makes hand signs. He reads your lips.

If you ask directly — "You are a bear of few words" — he responds by
placing "a closed fist over =pronouns.possessive= mouth, then taps
=pronouns.possessive= ear, then muzzle with one long finger."
*(conversation: Sparafucile)* Three gestures in a sequence: fist to
mouth, tap the ear, tap the muzzle. *Can't speak. Can't hear. Here is
where those things would be.* The diagnosis delivered by pointing to
the places of absence.

Ask if his condition came from the work — an accident, perhaps, with
powder or noise — and he shakes his head. Ask if it is lifelong, and
he nods. *(conversation: Sparafucile)* Born deaf and mute. The silence
is not a wound or an injury. It is the condition of the entire life.
And he confirms it with a small smile — the game describes him
"smiling a bit" as he nods — as though this is a simple fact about
himself, not a sorrow. *(conversation: Sparafucile — Disabearlity)*

Meanwhile, throughout every exchange, Sparafucile is watching your face.
When you continue talking to him, the description is exact: he waits
"keeping =pronouns.possessive= eyes focused on your lips."
*(conversation: Sparafucile)* He lip-reads. Every word you say passes
through your mouth's shape before it can become meaning for him. There
is an attention in that — a precise, sustained attention to the form of
language rather than its sound — that is its own kind of craft.

## [III. THE LANGUAGE — 9:00]

`[VISUAL: close on hands making gestures; then Sparafucile's tile; then
the Grit Gate interior.]`

The conversation with Sparafucile is, if you read it carefully, one of
the most expressive in the game. Nothing he does is casual. Every gesture
is deliberate — a specific thought translated into a specific physical
form. Three of them are worth dwelling on.

**The warning.** When you ask Sparafucile about a location the community
has reason to know well — a ruined place called Bethesda Susa, further
into the wilder parts of Qud — he does not simply gesture "dangerous." The node reads:

> *Sparafucile instructs you to wait, and spends a minute or so rifling*
> *through the papers and scraps scattered across =pronouns.possessive=*
> *workbench.*

Then:

> *At last =pronouns.subjective= produces a graphite sketch of a cylinder,*
> *an expertly-rendered specular revealing its surface to be shiny like*
> *glass. The detail of the cylinder's contents are indistinct and dark,*
> *but somehow menacing.*  
> *(conversation: Sparafucile)*

Then: "Sparafucile points to the glass and makes a breaking motion, then
draws a thumb across =pronouns.possessive= throat."
*(conversation: Sparafucile)*

A drawn cylinder with an expertly-rendered specular highlight. A
breaking motion. A finger across the throat. That is the warning — and
it is extraordinarily precise. He does not wave his hands and look
alarmed. He makes *something*, with graphite, on paper, well enough to
communicate "shiny glass, breakable, the contents are dangerous." The
warning is a document. The craftsman's instinct carries into everything
he communicates.

**The family sign.** When you ask whether he is happy living underground,
away from sun and open sky, he gives you:

> *Sparafucile gives you a warm smile and a firm nod. Pressing both*
> *pointers to their respective thumbs, =pronouns.subjective= taps these*
> *finger-circles together before =pronouns.possessive= chest and rotates*
> *both hands in place until the smallest of =pronouns.possessive= long*
> *fingers touch. Finally, =pronouns.subjective= gestures toward the other*
> *Barathrumites working at their stations.*  
> *(conversation: Sparafucile)*

Two finger-circles joined before the chest, rotated until the smallest
fingers meet — then a gesture outward toward the community around him.
The player option that reads it correctly is: "You are happy because you
are with your family?" *(conversation: Sparafucile)* And he nods. That
is the sign for family — not a universal sign language, but something
personal and invented, as eloquent as it needs to be. The underground
enclave, no sun, no open sky: happy, because the people are here.

**The farewell.** And then there is how he says goodbye. When you offer
the universal Qud farewell — "Live and drink, harm-artisan" — to someone
who cannot hear it, Sparafucile gives you this:

> *Sparafucile makes an 'L' shape with both hands, thumbs facing down and*
> *fingers pointed toward one another, before flipping both hands so*
> *=pronouns.possessive= thumbs face up. =pronouns.Subjective= then raises*
> *a closed fist to =pronouns.possessive= mouth, thumb out, and mimes*
> *drinking from it. This done, =pronouns.subjective= nods once more and*
> *turns back to =pronouns.possessive= work.*  
> *(conversation: Sparafucile)*

*Live* — the L-shapes, thumbs down and then flipped up, a gesture of
rising life. *And drink* — the closed fist at the mouth, thumb out,
miming the act. Then a nod, and back to work.

"Live and drink" is the whole social contract of Qud in three words —
the greeting, the farewell, the prayer, the thing you say to show
another person that you see them and wish them well. Sparafucile says it
in two hand gestures and a pantomime. It is the same as what you said.
It is precise enough to be understood. And then he turns back to his
workbench, because the work is always waiting.

## [IV. THE BEAR OF FEW WORDS — 13:30]

`[VISUAL: Sparafucile's tile; Shem-1.png; Neek.png; the Grit Gate
interior with Chromelings.]`

Now consider the range of expression within this silence.

The conversation does not represent Sparafucile as serene or placid. He
has a full palette, and it is worth cataloguing. When you patronize him
— when the player option chosen is "Yet you are an expert in your
field? How inspiring!" — the response is:

> *Sparafucile stares at you as if you have grown an additional,*
> *substantially uglier head, which you have not.*  
> *(conversation: Sparafucile)*

"Which you have not" is the game adding its own dry editorial comment.
The stare is the response of someone who does not need words to communicate
that they find you condescending and is not about to pretend otherwise.

When the player is clueless — when you say "I don't understand. Can you
explain aloud?" to a person who cannot speak aloud — he "averts
=pronouns.possessive= gaze and heaves a tired sigh." *(conversation:
Sparafucile)* This is the tiredness of someone who has been asked,
before, to simply use their voice. The sigh is not angry. It is tired.
There is a history in it that the game does not need to spell out.

When you apologize for that carelessness, he "gives you a wan smile and
a thumbs-up." *(conversation: Sparafucile)* The wan smile is not quite
forgiveness and not quite nothing — it is the acknowledgment of a well-
meaning person who made a predictable mistake. The thumbs-up says
*it's fine.* Together they say *I've seen this before, and you being
sorry is the right response.* Economy of expression, again.

And when you ask about Pax Klanq — a remarkable figure in the enclave,
hard to miss — Sparafucile "nods slowly, smirking." *(conversation:
Sparafucile)* Ask for advice about meeting Klanq and he "shrugs, still
smirking." *(conversation: Sparafucile)* The slow nod and the held smirk
across two separate nodes: there is something he knows about the
experience of meeting Pax Klanq, and he is amused at the question, and
he is not going to tell you what it is. That is wit. That is a personality.

Sparafucile is embedded in the life of the enclave. The robot Shem,
cataloguing his own history of growth and self-revision, mentions in
passing that one iteration ago he "climbed onto Sparafucile's workbench
to have my screws replaced." *(conversation: Shem)* The workbench is
not just for guns. Shem brings himself there. Neek, the community's
most excitable member, listing everyone who was part of the shared
experience of the cavern's vibration, goes through the roll: *"Mafeo.
Sparafucile. The chromelings. The rocks. Everyone else."*
*(conversation: Neek)* In a list of the community's present members,
Sparafucile is named the same way anyone else is named — not "the quiet
one" or "the deaf one" but just *Sparafucile,* second after Mafeo,
before the chromelings, part of the everyone.

## [V. THE CRAFTSMAN'S GIFT — 17:00]

`[VISUAL: the masterwork carbine tile; then Sparafucile's tile; then the
overworld of Grit Gate receding into the distance.]`

Return to the weapon. Otho hands it to you, says it bears the mark of
the finest gunsmith, and sends you on your way. You receive it before
you have ever spoken to Sparafucile — before you know anything about
who made it. It is the first gift from this community, and it is a
precise, made thing: a carbine or chain pistol that does what it should
do, crafted by someone who measures powder to the grain and spirals the
barrel until it is flawless.

In the enclave, Sparafucile is the one whose work you can hold. The
weapon in your hand is a product of one craftsman's judgment about when
a barrel is ready and when it is not — and that judgment is what a mark
means.

What Sparafucile cannot do — the words he cannot say, the sounds he
cannot hear — the game is very careful never to treat as a lack that
needs compensating for. He is not positioned as inspiring because he
overcame something. He is positioned as *the finest gunsmith,* full
stop. The mark on the weapon does not say *made by the deaf-mute
tinker;* it says *made by the best one we have.* Hortensa does not
describe him as admirable for what he manages despite his condition;
she describes the moment a perfect barrel comes out of his hands, and
the way his face lights up, and calls her view of that *precious.*

And then: he signs "live and drink" — the L-shapes and the mimed cup —
nods once, and turns back to his work. Because the next barrel needs
spiraling. Because the powder needs measuring. Because there is a mark
to maintain.

`[END CARD.]`

**Why this works as a standalone (and why it's the right length):**
- Single, vivid paradox as hook: the finest gunsmith cannot hear the
  guns fire; the most expressive character in the enclave never speaks.
- Complete arc: the weapon → the craftsman → the silence → the
  language → the community → what the mark means.
- No dependency on the main quest; the weapon and the enclave are
  context without requiring the player to know the Spindle arc.
- Intentionally a character study (~2800 words / ~19 min): Sparafucile's
  sourced lore is entirely in the conversation nodes (23 nodes, all
  gesture) plus five external references. The episode trades breadth
  for depth — it reads every gesture slowly rather than surveying a
  faction's history. Across the standalones it is the "individual
  craftsman" entry: where Golgotha is about a place's death, this is
  about one person's life in a community.
