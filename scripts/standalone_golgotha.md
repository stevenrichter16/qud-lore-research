# Golgotha: the Cloaca of the World

**A standalone deep-lore script for Caves of Qud.** A profile of a
single place — Qud's great trash-cavern, and the death that waits at
the bottom of it. Not part of the Resheph/Spindle series and distinct
from the faction, entity, and village one-offs. Self-contained; no
prior episodes required. Target runtime: 19-22 min · target
wordcount: ~3000 words. A leaner, atmospheric *place/horror* piece by
design — Golgotha's corpus footprint is small, so this is built from
the extracted corpus **and** the decompiled game data (both permitted
under the strict-evidence rule), and it leans into mood over breadth.

**Strict-evidence rule** (per `feedback_qud_script_strict_evidence`):
every lore claim comes from the extracted corpus or the decompile.
Quotes sourced; inference labeled; player-determined outcomes never
stated as single canon. The Caves of Qud wiki is used ONLY for the
downloaded visual assets at the end — never as a lore source.

## [COLD OPEN — 0:00]

`[VISUAL: the dark mouth of a cavern in the northern hills; then a
slow downward pan into trash chutes slick with slime.]`

Every world needs a place to put the things it can't look at — its
refuse, its dead, its filth. Most fantasy worlds bury that under a
cathedral or a treasure vault, so the deepest pit is also the most
glorious one. Caves of Qud does the opposite. The deepest, most
infamous descent near the heart of the early game leads not to a hoard
but to a *sewer* — a vast cavern of trash chutes that ends in a pit of
sludge, where the air itself will rot the tongue out of your mouth.

Its name is Golgotha. The people who have been there do not describe
it the way you describe an adventure. They describe it the way you
describe an illness:

> *"Golgotha is an execrable place in more ways than one, and I would*
> *advise you not to visit without a healthy stock of yuckwheat and*
> *honey. If you can cook, a meal with yuckwheat may banish a disease*
> *before it comes into its own. Bring salves or urberries, too, and*
> *mind your feet in the Cloaca."*  
> — *(conversation: Mafeo)*

*Mind your feet in the Cloaca.* This is an episode about the one place
in Qud where heroism gets you nothing but slime — the cloaca of the
world, what's down there, and the quiet horror of the death it offers.

`[TITLE CARD: GOLGOTHA — THE CLOACA OF THE WORLD.]`

## [I. THE DESCENT — 1:30]

`[VISUAL: a vertical cutaway — the cavern mouth up top, level after
level of trash chutes, the Cloaca at the bottom.]`

Golgotha is a great cavern in the north of Qud, and the first thing to
understand about it is the direction. You do not walk *into* Golgotha.
You go *down* it.

The place is built as a vertical shaft — in the game's own map data, a
stack of levels labeled, with no euphemism, the **trash chutes**, one
below the next, descending to a final floor named the **Cloaca.**
*(world: JoppaWorld — Golgotha cells)* That word is doing deliberate
work. A cloaca, in anatomy, is the single rear vent through which an
animal passes everything it needs to expel. Qud names the bottom of
its most famous dungeon after the body's waste-hole. The structure is
the thesis: Golgotha is a throat that only swallows, and the Cloaca is
where everything it swallows ends up.

What falls down those chutes is exactly what the name promises —
refuse, slime, the discarded and the dead, sliding down level by level
into the dark. And what lives down there has adapted to it. This is
not a place of treasure rooms and torch-lit halls. It is wet, it is
fouled, and it is actively hostile to the simple act of being a body
in it. The standing advice from anyone who knows it is not *bring a
better sword.* It is *bring medicine, and watch where you step.*

Because the real danger in Golgotha is not what bites you. It's what
you breathe, and what you wade through.

## [II. THE ROTTING TONGUE — 4:00]

`[VISUAL: the physician's compendium, Corpus Choliys, open to its
plague entries; then a slow push on a mouth held shut.]`

There is, in Qud, a physician's book — a forty-year compendium of
diseases called the *Corpus Choliys,* written by an apothecary named
Beniyayut Iym and copied out by the monks of the Monastery of the
Illustrious Heart of Chrome. It catalogues the afflictions of the
world the way a careful doctor would: symptoms, progression, remedy.
And one of its entries is the true reason Golgotha is feared. It is a
disease called **glotrot,** and the physician traces it to exactly one
place:

> *"Glotrot is an affliction characterized by necrosis of the tongue.*
> *Judging by evidence gathered from my patients' accounts, I place the*
> *disease's origin within the cavern of Golgotha where victims most*
> *likely contracted it from the putrid spawn of Agolgot."*  
> — *(book: Corpus Choliys)*

Necrosis of the tongue. Sit with what that actually means, because the
book does not flinch from it:

> *"The afflicted patient will complain of soreness in the throat as*
> *the tongue tissue begins to swell. Within a week's span, the muscle*
> *will soften and start to decompose. The patient will experience*
> *severe pain while ingesting food or drink and may agitate the last*
> *of the raw, living tissue, causing the mouth to bleed. Over the*
> *following few days, the tongue will rot in its entirety, rendering*
> *the patient incapable of speech."*  
> — *(book: Corpus Choliys)*

The game models this on the body, step by step. When you catch it, the
notice is plain: *"You have contracted glotrot! Your tongue begins to
bleed as the muscle rots away."* For a while the status reads *"Tongue
is rotting away. Starts bleeding when eating or drinking."* And then,
when it's finished, the status changes to four words that are quietly
one of the most horrifying outcomes in the game: *"Tongue has rotted
away. Can't speak."* *(game data: Glotrot effect)*

That is what Golgotha does to you. Not a glorious wound. It rots your
tongue out of your head and takes your *voice* — leaves you alive,
intact, and silent. In a world where the universal greeting, the
prayer, the whole social contract is the spoken phrase *"live and
drink,"* glotrot is a kind of social death: it makes you a person who
can no longer say the words.

It can be undone, but the cure is its own small nightmare. Caught
early, the physician writes, common remedies — *"yuckwheat, honey"* —
can reverse the necrosis. But once the tongue is entirely gone,
regrowing it is brutal:

> *"only the scarce ubernostrum tonic will cause it to regrow. However,*
> *simply injecting the tonic will merely regrow the tongue, which will*
> *proceed to rot away once more."*  
> — *(book: Corpus Choliys)*

First you have to purge the disease itself — by drinking a concoction
called the *Flaming Ick,* "drank from a flaming vessel" — and only
then can the new tongue take. *(book: Corpus Choliys)* You drink fire to burn the
rot out, and then you grow your voice back. That is the price of a trip
to Golgotha gone wrong.

## [III. THE CRADLE OF AGOLGOT — 9:00]

`[VISUAL: the lowest level — sludge wells in the floor; something vast
and still half-sunk in the muck.]`

The physician named a source for the rot: *"the putrid spawn of
Agolgot."* So who, or what, is Agolgot?

Agolgot is the thing at the bottom. Beneath the Cloaca, in the deepest
stratum of the cavern, the game builds a lair its own map-maker labels
**"the cradle of Agolgot"** — a chamber of sludge-wells and pitted
floor, a nursery sunk in muck. *(game data: Golgotha zone-builder —
the cradle of Agolgot)* Agolgot itself is a sleeper, and the
clearest description in the corpus comes secondhand, from a village
elder examining one of the creatures that crawled up out of it — a
slick, muscled thing that, in his words,

> *"...muscled out the bilge hose of sleeping Agolgot, in the cave*
> *under the Cloaca..."*  
> — *(conversation: Irudad)*

Read that image slowly. *The bilge hose of sleeping Agolgot.* Down at
the very bottom of the world's trash-shaft, beneath the sewage, there
is something so large and so alive that creatures are *born out of its
plumbing* — pushed out into the sludge like bilge-water out of a hull,
to crawl up the chutes carrying disease in their wake. Golgotha is not
just a place where filth collects. It is a place where filth is
*generating,* where the cradle at the bottom is always producing more.

The script will stay honest about its own limits here: the corpus and
the game data tell us *that* Agolgot sleeps in the cradle at the
bottom of Golgotha, *that* it spawns the creatures that breed there,
and *that* those spawn are the origin of glotrot. What Agolgot
ultimately *is* — its deeper nature in the wider story of Qud — the
game keeps for another, much larger thread, and this standalone leaves
it there, asleep in the muck, exactly as a traveler at the bottom of
the Cloaca would find it: enormous, breathing, and best not woken.

## [IV. THE EXECRABLE PILGRIMAGE — 13:00]

`[VISUAL: an adventurer climbing back out of the chutes, filthy and
shaking; then the same figure cleaned up, telling the tale.]`

So why does anyone go down there?

Because Golgotha, for all that it is a sewer, is also a *proving
ground* — the descent that travelers make to test themselves early in
their journey, and the one they brag about surviving. The game records
the deed in its legend-templates with a phrase that captures the whole
grim comedy of it: the hero who completes the descent is remembered as
having *"emerged from the trash chutes of Golgotha, victorious and
bathed in slime."* *(quest: More Than a Willing Spirit)*

*Victorious and bathed in slime.* That single line is the most Qud
thing imaginable, and it is worth dwelling on. Other worlds give you a
rite of passage that ends with you holding a glowing sword on a
mountaintop. Qud's early rite of passage ends with you climbing out of
a garbage chute, covered head to foot in filth, having maybe nearly
lost your tongue — and that *counts.* That is the victory. You went
into the cloaca of the world, you waded through the worst of what the
world discards, you did not catch the rot or you cured it, and you came
back up. Whether *you* make that descent, of course, is yours to
decide — but the legend the game keeps for those who do is honest about
the cost: it does not pretend you came back clean.

There's a strange dignity in that, buried under the slime. Golgotha
refuses to flatter the hero. It is the part of the adventure that is
not an adventure at all — that is just endurance, and disease, and
muck, and the simple animal achievement of going down into the worst
place and coming back up still able to speak. The world is full of
glorious things to do. Golgotha is the unglorious one you have to
survive first.

## [V. THE CLOACA OF THE WORLD — 16:30]

`[VISUAL: pull back up the chutes, out of the cavern mouth, into clean
northern air; hold on the dark opening below.]`

Set Golgotha next to the rest of Qud and you see what it's for. This
is a setting obsessed with height and ascent — spires that touch
heaven, dead god-kings entombed up in the light, the whole drama of the
world pointed *upward.* Golgotha is the counterweight, and it points
straight *down.* For everything Qud lifts toward the sky, there has to
be somewhere the opposite goes: the refuse, the runoff, the diseased,
the discarded. That place is Golgotha, and the game gives it the most
unsentimental name a body has — the cloaca.

What makes it genuinely frightening is not the monsters. It's the
*kind* of death it offers. Most dungeons threaten you with a dramatic
end — a blade, a fall, a beast. Golgotha threatens you with something
smaller and worse: it threatens to take your tongue and send you home
silent, alive but unable to say *live and drink* ever again; or to put
you in the sludge at the bottom, one more thing the world has expelled,
in the cradle of a sleeper that never stops breeding. It is a hell not
of fire and punishment but of *waste* — the horror of being reduced to
refuse, of dying not gloriously but discarded.

And then, when you've climbed back out into the clean air of the
northern hills, the cavern mouth is still there behind you, dark and
patient, taking in whatever the world drops down it. Something is still
asleep at the bottom. The chutes are still slick. And the next traveler
who wants to prove they're worth anything will have to go down into the
cloaca of the world, and come back up — if they can — bathed in slime.

`[END CARD.]`

**Why this works as a standalone (and why it's leaner):**
- A single, vivid hook (the dungeon that is a sewer; the death that
  takes your voice) and a complete arc (the descent → the disease →
  the sleeper → why anyone goes → what it means) in one place.
- No dependency on the main quest; the descent, the Cloaca, glotrot,
  and the cradle are all legible without the Gyre cosmology.
- It is intentionally shorter (~3000 words / ~20 min) than the
  society/character one-offs: Golgotha's sourced lore is genuinely
  sparse, so the episode trades breadth for mood — a horror/place piece
  rather than a deep social study. Across the standalones it is the
  "underworld" entry: where Bey Lah / mopango / Putus Templar / Chavvah
  / Joppa are about how people *live,* Golgotha is about where the
  world puts what it can't keep. (Cross-set framing for the PLAN/notes
  only; the episode stands alone.)
