# Barathrum the Old: The Bear Who Failed

**A deep-lore script for Caves of Qud.** Episode 2 in the series.
Target runtime: 20-22 min · target wordcount: ~3200 words.

Continuity: this episode assumes the viewer has seen Episode 1
("Resheph and the Plagues") and knows the basic shape of the reveal
— the Coven, the injunction, the Seraph atop the Spindle, the
plagues as engineered. This episode is about the *other* person in
that conversation: the bear who said yes.

Style guide for the narrator: patient, considered, willing to sit
with the moral weight. This is the saddest character in Caves of
Qud and the script should sound like it knows that. Quote in-game
text verbatim — bold text in the script is on-screen quote.
Production cues in `[BRACKETS]`.

---

## [COLD OPEN — 0:00]

`[VISUAL: an aged urshiib — a quilled albino bear — sitting in a
candle-dim study under the arch of Grit Gate. Books. A houseplant.
He is exhausted.]`

There is a candle-dim study under the ruins of Grit Gate. It
contains a houseplant called Euclid, whom Barathrum introduces
as *"a prattleplant. It stores every phrase it hears in its
neuroot network, and it mimics speech by flicking its leaves
against one another. What comes out is gibberish as often as
not, but even so, it's agreeable company for my nights spent
tinkering. It is old, perhaps older than I am, and it hasn't yet
run out of things to say."* *(conversation: Barathrum)*

In the same study, in Barathrum's late hidden conversation, the
player will find a torn graph-paper note about *deceit* and a
leather codex Barathrum calls *"a comfort, a guidebook, a ledger.
My crimes have no signatories, but the heart is its own
document."* *(hidden conversation: Barathrum)*

And there is a sound he hears that you do not. He calls it his
*tolling bell.* It is the Signal broadcasting from the top of the
Spindle. In his words: *"This racket of a Signal is mine, too, of
sorts. It is my tolling bell."*

This is Barathrum the Old. Argyve calls him *"the eldest and
wisest tinker alive."* *(conversation: Argyve)* He leads a guild
of disciples called the Barathrumites, mostly urshiib like him,
who live under the arch of Grit Gate.

And, as the late hidden conversation will reveal if you ascend
the Spindle and ask for the truth, he has been carrying a story
he never told his apprentices in full.

`[TITLE CARD: BARATHRUM THE OLD: THE BEAR WHO FAILED.]`

## [I. THE BEAR AT THE ARCH — 1:30]

`[VISUAL: Grit Gate from outside. The arch. The Barathrumites in
the workshops below.]`

The first thing you learn about Barathrum, in Joppa, from old
Argyve — who has known him longer than any human in the world —
is that you should go find him:

> *"You must seek out Barathrum the Old, the eldest and wisest*
> *tinker alive. He lives with his followers the Barathrumites*
> *under the arch of Grit Gate to the northeast, in the*
> *jungle-strangled ruins of Qud."*  
> — *(conversation: Argyve)*

And the second thing:

> *"Disciples of Barathrum. Mostly urshiib, like their mentor —*
> *quilled albino bears who prefer their candle-dim caves to the*
> *sun-salted surface of the jungle. A thousand years ago*
> *Barathrum and his kin crossed the Homs Delta…"*  
> — *(conversation: Argyve)*

The urshiib. Quilled albino bears. They prefer the dim places.
Their cave-dwelling is *cultural*, not just biological — they are
private people, slow people, scholarly people. Barathrum's
Barathrumites are mostly bears. His chief apprentices include
Hortensa and his protégé Q Girl, along with Otho. (Pax Klanq —
the eccentric mushroom prodigy who eventually builds the
Spindle-climber — is not a disciple but an outside contractor,
brought in years later on an old debt.) Some have studied under
him for decades. As Hortensa
says, *"Most of these wet-earred cubs were merely sketches in
their mothers' dreambooks when I arrived to study under
Barathrum."* *(conversation: Hortensa)* Centuries of teaching
under one bear.

When you finally meet him, he greets you like one of his own:

> *"Should you be here, =player.offspringTerm=?"*

*Offspring-term.* The terms by which an urshiib elder names a
young apprentice. He calls you *nestling.* And later, when you
return: *"Is that you, nestling =name=? Are you returned from
gate and Tomb? Come closer, I say."* *(conversation: Barathrum)*

This is the public Barathrum. Patient, slow, paternal. The bear
in his study. A figure who has survived a thousand years not by
ferocity but by stamina — by being there, holding the lamp lit,
waiting for someone to bring something back from the gate.

## [II. THE CUB WHO CROSSED — 4:00]

`[VISUAL: a glacial river roaring through a chrome grotto. The
ice unthawing.]`

If you ask Barathrum about himself — and his first instinct will
be to deflect onto the work — he can be coaxed into a particular
memory. He was a cub once.

> *"Little do I remember now, for I was but a cub, a nestling,*
> *when my kin and I crossed the Homs Delta into Qud. We were*
> *urged on by the flooding of a glacial river that flowed through*
> *our hearth-cave. The world was just unthawing then, and I*
> *recall the roar of the freezing water as it rushed through*
> *the grotto, and the frigid spray on my face as I watched in*
> *awe."*  
> — *(conversation: Barathrum)*

The world was unthawing. A glacial river ran through his hearth
cave, his family's home, and they were forced to flee. He
remembers the frigid spray on his face. That is the first thing
he remembers about himself.

`[VISUAL: hold on the image of frigid spray. Let it be cold.]`

He came to Qud as a refugee, with his clan. In his own words from
the late hidden conversation: *"when I, a young bruin, crossed
the Homs Delta with my flood-fleeing clan and into the western
reach of Qud, the plagues of the Gyre were already ascendant.
We took root at our new hearth, in the chrome grottos where we
urshiib find natural peace, and by the providence of my elders,
I was able to study the tinkering craft."*

This is the small detail that the long arc of his life will rest
on. He was a *promising apprentice.* He caught the eye of someone
more important than his clan-elders. A master machinist.

> *"Through my work I caught the attention of a master machinist,*
> *unconventional of method, who took me under her wing and, in*
> *time, availed me of the orphic truth."*

The master machinist was named Rebekah. She was, in our previous
episode, the one who would later be killed by the Seraph for
betraying his plan. In this episode, she is the woman who took a
refugee cub into her workshop and taught him how the world
actually worked.

## [III. THE ORPHIC TRUTH — 7:00]

`[VISUAL: Rebekah in her workshop. The Spindle on the horizon.]`

Barathrum gives his apprentices, including you, a *half-version*
of the orphic truth. He's told this version many times. It comes
out smooth:

> *"In the earliest aurora of our past, the Eaters of Earth were*
> *joined by a great coven of beings that spanned the firmament.*
> *But the Eaters succumbed to some terrible temptation, and an*
> *injunction was placed on our world."*  
> — *(conversation: Barathrum)*

The world once belonged to a great coven of star-spanning beings.
The Eaters of Earth had a covenant with them. The Eaters broke
faith somehow. The Coven left. An injunction was placed. The
stars went silent. The world was left "to molder and decay."

This much, his apprentices have been taught. It is the official
Barathrumite cosmology. The signal from the Spindle, which has
begun broadcasting in his late years, is — he says — proof the
injunction *may* be ending:

> *"The signal affirms that an entity roosts atop the Spindle, and*
> *invites us to join it. Dare I say, is the injunction at an*
> *end? It's too early to tell, but alas, there could be hope for*
> *our world."*

He says this with the careful brightness of a teacher who has
practiced saying it.

And then, if you ascend the Spindle and confront him with what
you find there, he will say more. The phrasing he uses:

> *"You see, =name=, when I, a young bruin, crossed the Homs*
> *Delta with my flood-fleeing clan and into the western reach*
> *of Qud, the plagues of the Gyre were already ascendant."*  
> *"This, you know. It is the truth I told, if only half. But*
> *Rebekah taught me more."*

*The truth I told, if only half.* For a thousand years, Barathrum
has been teaching his apprentices a partial truth. The full truth
is that the Coven didn't merely *depart.* Atop the Spindle, the
Coven left a Seraph behind. And the Seraph, in the brief years
before Barathrum's clan arrived in Qud, had decided the world's
inhabitants needed to be removed.

> *"To ready our world for the Coven's return, the Seraph, who*
> *would be called Resheph, became convinced that all higher life*
> *on the planet must... be purged."*  
> — *(hidden conversation: Barathrum)*

Rebekah served that Seraph. In Barathrum's exact phrasing from
the late hidden conversation: *"Rebekah, dear tutor, betrayed
Resheph's confidences and apprised me of the plan, so that I
might help her dash it apart and save the peoples of our world."*

The source does not narrate Barathrum's deliberation, only his
choice: he and Rebekah went together to confront the Seraph.
*"Together we beseeched Resheph, begged them to reverse the
Gyre. I pleaded.. I-"* (he wheezes and cannot finish that
sentence in the hidden conversation.)

## [IV. THE BARGAIN — 11:00]

`[VISUAL: a council of three. The Seraph at the apex. Rebekah and
Barathrum on the ground.]`

What happens next we covered in Episode 1, but it's worth
revisiting from Barathrum's side of the table.

The two of them — a master machinist and her apprentice — went
to the top of the Spindle to confront a Seraph. There was, as
Barathrum recalls, *"a storm of shrapnel words."* The Seraph
acceded to a thousand years.

> *"Ten centuries! An eyeblink on the scales of the Folk Clock*
> *but an eternity for us. So I believed."*  
> *"The rousing Signal would cease, the plagues of the Gyre would*
> *be reversed, to the extent they could; for even the potent*
> *archon had not full governance over what they had worked into*
> *being."*

And here is the line that should sit cold:

> *"We allied on the notion that the sultanate must be abolished*
> *for the future to flower. I was to execute the earthly plan,*
> *while Resheph continued preparations at the astronomical*
> *tier."*

Barathrum agreed to execute the earthly plan. Not to *stop*
Resheph. To *do part of his work.* The thousand-year stay of
execution was contingent on the Sultanate being dismantled, on
Qud being remade into something the Coven might find acceptable.
Resheph, the would-be exterminator, became the project's partner.
Rebekah was the bridge between them. Barathrum was, by his own
phrasing, the *earthly executor.*

And the moment that gives the whole script its shape — the
sentence that the player only learns by climbing to the top of
the Spindle and asking for the truth:

> *"To achieve a sort of bicameralism of action, Resheph insisted*
> *on reformatting their personality as a triumvirate, seeded*
> *from thin scans of the original archon, Rebekah, and myself."*

A thin scan of Barathrum's mind was wedded to a Seraph who had
chosen to commit planetary genocide. Barathrum's character — *some
slice* of it — became part of the personality that the Mechanimist
faith would later worship as Resheph. The game text does not tell
you which of the three personae (the Healer, the Coiled Lamb, the
Above) the Barathrum-scan ended up wearing. The inference is
tempting, especially for the Coiled Lamb, but the source declines
to spell it out.

What the source *does* spell out is the next line:

> *"The art of mythmaking was employed to enshrine the Resheph*
> *persona. Thus- the healer, the Coiled Lamb, and perhaps*
> *unexpectedly, the Above."*

The myth was built by the people the myth was about. Including
him.

`[VISUAL: a sermon. A Mechanimist preacher invokes Resheph. Hold
on Barathrum's face hearing it.]`

## [V. A THOUSAND YEARS OF SHIM AND SOLDER — 14:30]

`[VISUAL: time-lapse. Centuries of Barathrumite workshops. Tools
worn down. New apprentices. Old apprentices buried.]`

For a thousand years, Barathrum carried it.

He worked. He built the Barathrumites. He taught Hortensa for
centuries. He took on Q Girl, and Otho, and the rest. He
maintained a public posture as a wise tinker in a candle-dim
study, beloved of his apprentices, a private scholar in the chrome
grottos.

The work of the *earthly plan*, as he names it in his late
breakdown:

> *"I tried... for decades on decades I tried our paws on the*
> *tiller, to shim together what blossomed in the wild... The*
> *restoration of machines, the federation of free sovranties,*
> *the nourishing rituals of salt and star, the sacred, the*
> *profane..."*  
> — *(hidden conversation: Barathrum)*

Restoration of machines. Federation of free sovranties. The
rituals of salt and star. The sacred *and* the profane. The
project was *to lift Qud across the eschaton.* To make it the
kind of world a Coven would not, when it returned, sterilize.

Centuries pass. He watches the river of Time, as he says in one
of his rare gentle moments, *"turn the gears of Qud."* Apprentices
come and go. He buries some of them.

> *"For a millennium now I've watched the river of Time turn the*
> *gears of Qud. I even played my part in diverting the river*
> *where I could. Oh, but there have been so many players through*
> *the years! So many players."*  
> — *(conversation: Barathrum)*

So many players. He has watched many of you walk past Grit Gate.

And slowly, by his own admission, the project failed:

> *"No... Qud is fractious, and no lasting peace has obtained."*  
> *"In the late years I saw the writing on the wall. I turned us*
> *inward, retreated to my candle-dim study to angle at the*
> *project alone."*

The Putus Templar persist. The factions schism. The free
sovranties prove un-federable. The rituals of salt and star
remain ritual, not transformation. A thousand years of patient
tinkering, of shim and solder, of running a guild from a study
under a ruined arch — and Qud is *fractious.*

The Signal, the rousing wave from the top of the Spindle, begins
to broadcast again. In Barathrum's account, the original accord
specified that the Signal *would cease* during the thousand-year
stay: *"The rousing Signal would cease, the plagues of the Gyre
would be reversed, to the extent they could; for even the potent
archon had not full governance over what they had worked into
being."* So the Signal's *return*, by Barathrum's own framing, is
evidence the accord is ending.

He hears it. He calls it *his tolling bell.*

## [VI. THE BREAKDOWN — 18:00]

`[VISUAL: Barathrum in late game. Hunched. Whimpering. The lamps
flicker.]`

If you make it to the late hidden conversation, after you have
ascended the Spindle and turned the Gyre, Barathrum will let
go. The dialogue, transcribed verbatim from the file the game
flags `ExcludeFromCorpusGeneration='true'`:

> *Barathrum heaves a great sigh upon the moist air of the*
> *control pit. Barathrum stumbles to the floor with paws to his*
> *face.*  
> *"I failed it! I have failed! A thousand years too short, and-*
> *MORE TIME"*  
> *Barathrum weeps.*

He asks for *more time.* He has just had a thousand years.

`[VISUAL: hold on the figure on the floor. Don't rush.]`

The dialogue then degenerates into a strange register. He
invokes geographical features as if they were gods. The Winding
Svy — a river. Lake Hinnom — a lake. The Moon. The Sun. He
addresses them directly:

> *"Winding Svy? Thou art devious in symbol shape like serpent*
> *Ba'al!"*  
> *"Hinnom? For blooming alga thou crossed thy perch's back!"*  
> *"Shiftless moon?"*  
> *"Sun! Proud and idle, Sun! Silver father of the protoplanets!*
> *Claim mine selfhood and be done with it."*  
> *Barathrum weeps.*

The line that names what he has been doing for a thousand years
follows immediately:

> *"Then? Perhaps.. Perhaps the greater bodies whose agency I*
> *share the smaller cut from might share too the greater portion*
> *of my meal in blame."*

*The greater portion of my meal in blame.* Barathrum has, by his
own phrasing, been *eating the blame.* He held the full truth
(per his earlier line, *"It is the truth I told, if only half"*).
He let the Mechanimists worship Resheph without correction. The
source does not say in so many words that this concealment was
deliberate strategy versus simple exhaustion — only that it
happened, and that he now speaks of it as *crimes* with the heart
as their document.

## [VII. THE STARSHIIB — 21:00]

`[VISUAL: Barathrum's face suddenly burning bright. He laughs.
The lamps come back up.]`

There is one more beat. After the breakdown — after the
invocations to Svy and Hinnom and the Sun — Barathrum's
countenance, in the script's stage direction, *"suddenly burns
bright."*

He has remembered something.

> *"Did you know, =name=, I harbor a memory older than even the*
> *flooding of my hearth cave. A gleaming ancient tale, told by*
> *my elders to the nippy cub I once was..."*  
> *"In the age of the covenate, a coterie of our urshiib*
> *ancestors took to the stars to ply their burning cores! Can*
> *you envision it??"*

Urshiib in space. Star-bears. Barathrum at his most undone is
remembering a cub's tale his elders told him in the chrome
grotto, before the world unthawed, before he became the executor
of an earthly plan that didn't take.

> *"Ho, ho! I know a secret! Heh, heh! I know... there is a*
> *functioning starship atop the Spindle. We can take it, =name=!*
> *We launch ourselves into the deepness of the dusted cosmos and*
> *find the starshiib! You and I, ho ho!"*  
> *"Starshiib!"*

He wants to *leave.*

`[VISUAL: hold on Barathrum's bright face.]`

What the source establishes: there is a functioning starship atop
the Spindle. Barathrum has known about it (his line: *"I know a
secret! Heh, heh! I know..."*). The urshiib cub's tale he was
told featured ancestors who took to the stars in the *age of the
covenate.* He proposes to escape rather than continue the earthly
project.

> *"Starshiib! Starshiib! Starshiib! Starshiib! Starshiib!*
> *Starshiib! Starshiib! Starshiib! Starshiib! Starshiib!*
> *Starshiib! Starshiib! Starshiib! Starshiib! Starshiib!*
> *Starshiib!"*

In the actual conversation file, the word repeats sixteen times.
The last dialogue node is *just* the word *Starshiib*, capitalized,
chanted. The wisest tinker alive, after the breakdown, after the
invocations to the Sun, has reverted to a cub's word. He just
wants to find the bears who flew away.

---

## [VIII. THE QUESTION — 24:00]

`[VISUAL: black. The bear's silhouette against the Spindle.]`

*Caves of Qud* lets the player decide Barathrum's fate, not the
game's writers. The Resheph endgame conversation gates on a
delimited state — `(Barathrum:Dead OR Barathrum:Launched)` — used
to retarget dialogue toward a `BarathrumLives` branch
*(conversation: Resheph, hidden)*. In other words, the game tracks
three outcomes: Barathrum lives, Barathrum dies, or Barathrum
launches on the starship. The conversation does not foreclose any
of those.

Nor does the game adjudicate his choices for the player. It does
not tell the viewer whether allying with the Seraph was right.
It does not tell the viewer whether the thousand-year work was
honorable or a long collaboration in someone else's atrocity. The
two cases sit beside each other in the same conversation file:

> *"by the providence of my elders, I was able to study the*
> *tinkering craft."*

— a young refugee taken in, lifted by patient teachers.

> *"My crimes have no signatories, but the heart is its own*
> *document."*

— an old bear's leather codex on his bedroll.

What the script gives the player, by the end, is a thousand-year
old man on the floor of a study, weeping, chanting *Starshiib*
sixteen times. The game leaves the meaning open.

`[VISUAL: hold on the Spindle silhouetted against starfield.
Hold past the comfortable cut. Cut.]`

In the next video, we'll talk about Rebekah — the master machinist
who chose the harder path, and the Daughters of Exile she left
behind to carry her work after her death.

`[END CARD.]`

**Tone reminders:**
- Stay strictly inside source. Anything beyond quoted/paraphrased
  in-game text or directly observable code is OFF-LIMITS. If the
  source is silent, the script is silent (or flags the silence).
  See `feedback_qud_script_strict_evidence.md` memory.
- Specifically: the triumvirate-to-personas mapping is unspecified
  in the source; the script must not name which Resheph-persona
  was Barathrum's. The script may *raise* the inference and
  immediately note that the source declines to confirm it.
- Barathrum's fate is player-determined: the Resheph hidden
  conversation gates on `(Barathrum:Dead OR Barathrum:Launched)`
  vs a `BarathrumLives` branch (in `conversations_hidden/Resheph.md`,
  NOT the Barathrum file). Do not state a single outcome.
- The breakdown section (VI) must be paced *slow.* The lines
  *"more time"* and *"the greater portion of my meal in blame"*
  are the emotional core. Don't rush past them.
- The Starshiib section (VII) is tonally tricky. The narrator's
  voice should be quiet enough that the *Starshiib! Starshiib!*
  chant lands as comic-tragic, not just comic.
- The Question (VIII) must not editorialize beyond what the
  source supports.
