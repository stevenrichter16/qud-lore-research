# The Mopango: The Buried Watchers

**A standalone deep-lore script for Caves of Qud.** Not part of the
Resheph/Spindle series, and a different faction from the Bey Lah
one-off. Fully self-contained; no prior episodes required. Target
runtime: 21-24 min · target wordcount: ~3500 words.

**Strict-evidence rule** (per `feedback_qud_script_strict_evidence`):
every lore claim comes from the extracted corpus. Quotes sourced;
inference labeled; player-determined outcomes never stated as a
single canon. The Caves of Qud wiki is used ONLY for the downloaded
visual assets at the end — never as a lore source.

A note on pronouns: the mopango use *ey/em/eir* for everyone, and so
will this script when referring to individual mopango. That choice
is itself part of the lore — see §IV.

## [COLD OPEN — 0:00]

`[VISUAL: descent into a deep catacomb (07_mopango/...); a small,
brightly-painted turret turns to face you and, instead of firing,
speaks.]`

Deep under an ancient tomb, past the things that want to kill you,
there is a chain turret. Turrets in Caves of Qud are wall-mounted
guns; they shoot you. This one turns toward you, crackles, and says:

> *"By Her Light, [traveler]. An [thou wouldst] rest peaceably,*
> *[thou'rt] welcome here."*  
> — *(conversation: Vivira)*

The turret's name is Vivira, and behind em is a settlement of some
of the gentlest people in the entire game: the mopango. They are
plated, digging creatures whose armor scales glow a pale white;
they are, in one outsider's description, *"contemplative, sociable,
and mostly peaceful."* *(conversation: Zothom)* They live in the
deep dark of a tomb by choice, they make their decisions without a
single leader, they have quietly abolished gender, they build
wheelchairs for their disabled, and each of them lives by a personal
motto that it is rude to ask them to explain.

This is an episode about the mopango — the buried watchers — and
about what a society looks like when you build it not out of fear,
but out of humility, curiosity, and care.

`[TITLE CARD: THE MOPANGO: THE BURIED WATCHERS.]`

## [I. THE WATCHERS OF THE TOMB — 1:30]

`[VISUAL: the mopango settlement nestled in catacomb walls; plated,
glowing figures.]`

You reach the mopango the way most good things in Qud are reached:
by accident, and on someone's recommendation. A reformed graverobber
named Zothom — who tried to plunder this tomb and watched his
companions die — points the way; the mopango, he says, *"kept me
safe and fed me while I treated my wounds."* *(conversation: Zothom)*
At the entrance
to their settlement sits Vivira, the friendly turret, who explains:

> *"this is the Tomb of the Eaters, but behind me lieth nestled a*
> *settlement occupied by the watchers of the tomb, a small coterie*
> *of mopango."*  
> — *(conversation: Vivira)*

*The watchers of the tomb.* That phrase is the whole identity. Ask
any mopango who they are, and you get a version of the same answer.
Vivira's:

> *"Mopango are witnesses of the past and shepherds of the future.*
> *We seek peace and harmony through understanding the Kasaphescence*
> *and Her many children. We nonetheless eschew conformity, and each*
> *of us seeketh a personal credo by which we livest our lives."*  
> — *(conversation: Vivira)*

Witnesses of the past. Shepherds of the future. They have planted
themselves in the deepest, oldest place they could find, and made
their entire purpose the keeping of what came before. A watcher
named Agyra — the coterie's most enthusiastic host, who *"loveth the
sharing of knowledge"* — adds the practice that goes with the creed:
*"we commune with relics of antiquity to maintain our humility and
grow our wisdom."* *(conversation: Agyra)*

Note that word — *humility.* Most cultures study the past to claim
it. The mopango study it to stay small before it.

## [II. BY HER LIGHT — 4:00]

`[VISUAL: a mopango glowing softly; the pale-white scale-light.]`

The mopango greeting — *"By Her Light"* — points at their faith.
They worship something called the **Kasaphescence**, a feminine
divine principle whose light, they believe, is *within* them.
Agyra lays out the theology:

> *"The Sacred Light of the Kasaphescence shineth within us... We*
> *believe that one day, with enough knowledge, anyone who seeketh*
> *the Light shalt find it within emself."*  
> — *(conversation: Agyra)*

This is a quietly radical religion. The Light is not in a temple, a
relic, or a prophet. It is *latent in everyone*, and the path to it
is not obedience or sacrifice but *knowledge.* Salvation, for a
mopango, is an epistemology: study enough, understand enough, grow
humble and wise enough, and the light you're looking for turns out
to have been inside you the whole time. They don't even claim a
monopoly on it — *anyone* who seeks the Light can find it. A faith
with no chosen people.

When a mopango wishes you well, the blessing is *"May [thou] bathe
ever in Her light"* — and a delighted mopango is described, in the
game's own stage directions, as *glowing softly.* *(conversation:
Agyra)* The pale-white glow of their scales is not just biology in
the lore's framing; it's the visible sign of the thing they're all
reaching for.

## [III. NO ONE LEADS — 7:00]

`[VISUAL: mopango in discussion; a wheeled mopango; ey/em pronouns
on screen.]`

Ask to speak to whoever is in charge of the mopango and you will be
gently corrected. Agyra:

> *"No one leadeth us. We mopango hold little regard for hierarchy,*
> *and our shared decisions are made through discussion, quiet*
> *consideration, and consensus."*  
> — *(conversation: Agyra)*

No leader. No Hindriarch, no Sultan, no chief. Decisions by
discussion and consensus. And when the player presses — *shouldn't
there be a leader?* — Agyra simply asks back, *"Shouldst be
otherwise?"* and smiles, glowing softly. The mopango do not argue
for their anarchism; they just live it and find it obvious.

The same flatness extends to gender. Every mopango uses the same
pronouns — *ey, em, eir, eirs, emself* — and Agyra explains why
without a trace of defensiveness:

> *"Sex is personal and mattereth not to a bare acquaintence.*
> *Gender is a societal relic in which we see no current utility."*  
> — *(conversation: Agyra)*

A *societal relic* — exactly the sort of thing a people who study
relics for a living would recognize and set down.

And the care goes further than ideas. Some mopango have wheels.
Asked why, Agyra answers plainly: *"Maladies of the bone are not
uncommon in our people. We build devices to assist friends who
wouldst otherwise struggle to walk."* *(conversation: Agyra)*
Vivira the turret is the same principle taken to its limit — a
mopango mind whose friends, in eir own telling, *"uplifted me by
chance and cared for me by choice,"* and gave em a chassis to live
in. *(conversation: Vivira)* A society organized around what each
member needs, not what each member is.

That openness extends even to membership. The mopango will adopt
outsiders who wish to join — *"if these slynth wish to become*
*mopango... then we shall accept them. Let them gaze into the*
*schematics that wrote history beside us if they so choose."*
*(conversation: Agyra)* You are not born mopango. You become one,
by choosing to watch.

## [IV. THE CREDO — 10:30]

`[VISUAL: three mopango, each with their credo on screen as they
speak it.]`

Here is the idea at the center of mopango life, and one of the most
distinctive concepts in all of Caves of Qud: the *credo.* Every
mopango seeks a personal one. Agyra defines it:

> *"One's own credo shouldst inspire reflection and thought, with no*
> *clear-cut meaning but meaning nonetheless."*  
> — *(conversation: Agyra)*

A credo is a single sentence — closer to a koan than a motto — that
the mopango adopts as a lens for living. It is supposed to *mean
something* and to *resist easy explanation* at the same time. And
the mopango will share theirs freely. Vivira, the turret, offers:

> *"Form needeth not follow function."*  
> — *(conversation: Vivira)*

A weapon whose credo is that it need not be defined by being a
weapon. Agyra, the hospitable seeker of knowledge:

> *"Malice alone staineth the sanctity of questioning."*  
> — *(conversation: Agyra)*

A watcher named Doyoba, who tends a being trapped in stillness:

> *"Suffering breedeth in still water."*  
> — *(conversation: Doyoba)*

Each credo fits its speaker so well that you immediately want to ask
what it means. And that is the one thing you must not do. Asking a
mopango to explain eir credo is, in Agyra's words, *"one of our few
taboo acts."* The reason is the whole philosophy in miniature:

> *"A credo informeth and confoundeth at once, and must be reflected*
> *upon by the listener... I cannot simply explain my credo to*
> *[thee]."*  
> — *(conversation: Agyra)*

The point of a credo is not the answer. It is the *reflection it
provokes in you.* To explain it would be to do your thinking for
you — and a people whose entire faith is "seek the Light within
yourself" will not rob you of the seeking. When the player blunders
and asks Doyoba what eir credo means, the rebuke is perfect: *"Wouldst
I shall chew [thy] food for [thee] as well?"* *(conversation:
Doyoba)* — though ey, like every mopango, immediately forgives the
outsider who didn't know better.

## [V. WATCHING THE PAST — 14:00]

`[VISUAL: a mopango pressing its plated hands to an ancient relic; a
flash of vision.]`

So what does it actually mean to be a *watcher?* The mopango don't
just guard old things. They *commune* with them. Each watcher takes
a particular charge — *"each of us a different facet,"* Doyoba says —
and reads the past out of ancient relics through something like
psychometry. When Doyoba communes with a strange recovered device,
the experience is physical and painful:

> *"By her light, that was unpleasant. How it twisted and turned,*
> *leading me to visions of [the past], scraps and flashes. I can*
> *discern only glimpses of significance."*  
> — *(conversation: Doyoba)*

This is the labor behind the lofty title. "Watching the past" is not
contemplation in an armchair; it is pressing your mind against an
object that has outlived everything, taking on its scraps and
flashes, and bearing the weight of what you find. One mopango, a
reader named Lebah — *"the most insightful reader among us,"* who
*"beareth the weight of an anxious mind"* — communed with one
particular object and was so shaken that ey *"hath spent less time
away from eir nook than ever."* *(conversation: Agyra)* The mopango
take psychic damage, in effect, so that the past is not forgotten.

It is worth saying plainly what kind of courage this is. The
mopango are, by their own account, most comfortable *"in close
quarters... the close embrace of the depths."* *(conversation:
Agyra)* They chose the dark. They chose the dead things. They chose
to spend their lives pressing their minds against grief and ruin —
not to conquer it, but to witness it, so that it has at least one
set of eyes still on it.

## [VI. THE CHILDREN OF THE TOMB — 17:30]

`[VISUAL: the confined "children" — ancient beings the mopango watch
over; Doyoba beside eir charge.]`

The deepest expression of the mopango's care is what they call *the
children.* Down in the catacombs are ancient beings — older than the
mopango themselves — that have been confined for thousands of years.
The mopango watch over them, name them, and tend them. Doyoba
explains the affectionate misnomer:

> *"I am Doyoba, proud watcher of the children of the tomb. One*
> *child in particular. Of course [thou] must comprehend, 'child'*
> *is but a fabricated epithet, as they are far older than we."*  
> — *(conversation: Doyoba)*

Doyoba's charge is a being called Nacham — *"that which watcheth and*
*understandeth not, that which speaketh and cannot be understood.*
*Information floweth through Nacham as water through a sieve."*
*(conversation: Doyoba)* The mopango don't fully understand the
children. They watch them anyway. They gave a confined, incoherent,
ancient thing a *name* and a keeper who is *excited* to learn from
it — *"I cannot wait to speak to it."*

The player can, through the mopango, change one of these beings'
fates — recovering a device that, by one watcher's claim, *"grants
freedom,"* and choosing how to apply it. *(conversation: Agyra)*
Which child is freed, and whether freedom is even a kindness, is
left to the player; the mopango themselves are uncertain. When
Doyoba communes with the device, ey is left wondering whether
freeing a being is restoration or loss: *"An [thou sendest] void*
*away, hast [thou] lost a part of [thee]?"* *(conversation: Doyoba)*
The script will not pretend there is one right answer here — the
game doesn't offer one.
What the game offers is a people who looked at the oldest, most
forgotten prisoners in the world and decided that the right response
was not fear, not exploitation, but *attention.* They named the
forgotten and sat with them in the dark.

---

## [VII. THE QUESTION — 21:00]

`[VISUAL: the glowing settlement in the deep; pull back into the
dark; hold.]`

The mopango are a small faction. You can miss them entirely. They
guard nothing you can sell, rule nothing you can conquer, and want
nothing from you but good questions. And yet they may be the
clearest picture Caves of Qud offers of a *good* society — not a
utopia, just a working one, built on a coherent set of choices.

They abolished hierarchy and govern by consensus. They abolished
gender as a category they found no use for. They build for the
bodies their members actually have. They let anyone join who wishes
to, and they let anyone reach their Light who seeks it. They take on
the unpaid, painful, unending work of remembering — pressing their
minds against ancient grief so that the past keeps one witness. And
they organize their inner lives around a single sentence each, a
credo meant not to be explained but to be *lived with* — because a
people who believe the Light is found by seeking will not do your
seeking for you.

Agyra's own credo was *"Malice alone staineth the sanctity of
questioning."* The script will obey the taboo and not explain it.
But notice what it implies: that questioning is *sacred*, and that
the only thing that can spoil a question is ill will. A whole
civilization can be read out of that one sentence — a people for
whom curiosity is holy, hospitality is reflexive, and the only sin
is malice. They live in a tomb, in the dark, among the dead and the
forgotten, and they are, somehow, among the kindest people in Qud.

May you bathe ever in Her light.

`[END CARD.]`

  clear arc (arrival → understanding → the choice about the children)
  in one location.
